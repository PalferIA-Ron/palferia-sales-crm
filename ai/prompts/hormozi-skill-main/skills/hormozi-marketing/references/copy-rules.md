# Copy Rules & Framework Reference
## The Definitive Copy Audit & Voice Guide for Hormozi-Based Marketing

**Audience**: Spanish-speaking entrepreneurs building high-ticket sales funnels
**Purpose**: Systematic copywriting framework based on Alex Hormozi's proven principles
**Last Updated**: 2026-04-06

---

## 1. The Copy Audit Checklist (Auditoría de Copy)
### A Systematic 40-Point Framework for Any Marketing Copy

Use this checklist to systematically review sales pages, emails, landing pages, and funnels. Score each section and apply the scoring rubric at the end.

---

### Section 1: Value Equation Score (Puntuación de Ecuación de Valor)
The Value Equation framework: (Dream Outcome × Probability of Success) / (Time Required × Effort/Sacrifice Required) = VALUE

**Check 1: Is the Dream Outcome Specific and Measurable?**
- ✓ Good: "Get your first $10K client in 90 days"
- ✗ Bad: "Become more successful"
- Ask: Can someone measure whether they achieved it?

**Check 2: Is It the REAL Dream Outcome (Not a Proxy)?**
- ✓ Good: "Close sales 40% faster without discount wars"
- ✗ Bad: "Learn advanced negotiation techniques" (this is a proxy; the real outcome is closing more sales)
- Ask: Would a customer buy THIS or the outcome THAT delivers?
- The real outcome is always the change in their life/business, not the educational component.

**Check 3: Are There At Least 3 Proof Elements?**
Proof elements include: testimonials, case studies, data, credentials, results, awards, industry recognition, notable clients.
- ✓ Good: Testimonial + case study + quantified data
- ✗ Bad: One testimonial only
- More types of proof = more conviction

**Check 4: Is the Guarantee Strong Enough to Eliminate Risk Completely?**
- ✓ Good: "30-day money-back guarantee. Get access, implement, and if you don't get [specific result], full refund. No questions asked."
- ✗ Bad: "Satisfaction guaranteed" (too vague)
- ✗ Bad: 7-day guarantee (too short for complex results)
- Rule: The guarantee should be longer than the minimum time needed to see results.

**Check 5: Is the Timeframe to Results Clearly Stated?**
- ✓ Good: "In the next 90 days, you'll..."
- ✓ Good: "By week 3, most students..."
- ✗ Bad: "Eventually you'll see results"
- Specific timeframes create urgency and eliminate "when?" questions

**Check 6: Does It Feel Faster Than Alternatives?**
- ✓ Good: "3x faster than traditional sales coaching" OR "6 weeks instead of 12 months"
- ✓ Good: "While others are still studying, you'll already be selling"
- ✗ Bad: No comparative speed claim
- People want speed. Make them feel they're getting it.

**Check 7: Is the Effort/Sacrifice Minimized or Eliminated?**
- ✓ Good: "Just 30 minutes per day" OR "Done-for-you templates included"
- ✓ Good: "No technical skills required"
- ✗ Bad: "You'll need to grind hard"
- Frame effort positively: "Only need to..." rather than "You'll have to..."

**Check 8: Does It Position as Done-for-You or Easy Rather Than Hard Work?**
- ✓ Good: "Everything is already built. Just follow the system."
- ✓ Good: "We've removed the complexity."
- ✗ Bad: "You'll learn to build this yourself" (sounds like work)
- Note: If you can't make it easy/DFY, reframe as "following a proven step-by-step system"

---

### Section 2: Hook & Headline (El Gancho)
Your hook has 2-3 seconds to stop the scroll.

**Check 9: Would You Stop Scrolling for This Hook?**
- Read it aloud to 5 strangers. Would they keep reading?
- If you feel awkward reading it, it's not good enough.

**Check 10: Is It Under 10 Words?**
- ✓ Good: "Get your first $10K client in 90 days" (9 words)
- ✓ Good: "How to triple sales without hiring" (7 words)
- ✗ Bad: "The comprehensive guide to understanding the psychology of sales and negotiation in the modern B2B landscape" (way too long)
- Rule: If it doesn't fit comfortably in a headline, it's not focused enough.

**Check 11: Does It Contain a Specific Number or Metric?**
- ✓ Good: "7-figure sales skill" OR "40% increase in close rate"
- ✓ Good: "The 3-step formula"
- ✗ Bad: "How to increase sales significantly"
- Numbers are 2-3x more memorable than words.

**Check 12: Does It Target a Specific Person (Not Everyone)?**
- ✓ Good: "For coaches who want to charge $5K per client"
- ✓ Good: "If you've been stuck at $50K/year..."
- ✗ Bad: "For entrepreneurs" (too broad)
- Specific targeting = higher relevance = more stops

**Check 13: Does It Create a Curiosity Gap?**
A curiosity gap exists when you hint at something without fully explaining it.
- ✓ Good: "The one thing nobody tells you about sales"
- ✓ Good: "What top 1% closers know that you don't"
- ✗ Bad: Complete explanation in the headline
- The gap should make them NEED to know more.

**Check 14: Is the First Word a Power Word?**
- ✓ Good: Discover, Unlock, Steal, The Secret, Finally, Imagine, How
- ✗ Bad: Consider, Explore, Understand
- First word sets tone. Make it bold.

---

### Section 3: Offer Presentation (La Propuesta)
The offer is your deliverable. It must be crystal clear.

**Check 15: Is the Offer Clearly Stated (Not Buried)?**
- ✓ Good: "You're getting a $97 training bundle with..."
- ✗ Bad: Offer is mentioned in passing mid-copy
- Rule: State offer by 20% through the copy, at latest.

**Check 16: Are All Components Listed with Individual Perceived Values?**
- ✓ Good:
  ```
  Video Training Library ($497 value)
  + Email Templates ($197 value)
  + Private Slack Community ($997 value)
  Total value: $1,691
  ```
- ✗ Bad: "You get training materials and access to community"
- Each item should answer: "Why is this worth $X?"

**Check 17: Is There a Value Stack Showing Total Value vs. Price?**
- ✓ Good: "Total value: $2,847 | Your investment today: $297 | You save: $2,550"
- ✗ Bad: Just stating the price
- The gap between value and price should feel like a steal.

**Check 18: Are Bonuses Solving Specific Objections?**
- ✓ Good: "Bonus: Done-for-you email sequence (solves 'I don't have time' objection)"
- ✓ Good: "Bonus: 90-day money-back guarantee (solves 'Will this work for me?' objection)"
- ✗ Bad: Random bonuses that don't address pain points
- Every bonus should answer an unspoken question.

**Check 19: Is the Guarantee Prominently Placed?**
- ✓ Good: Big, bold box BEFORE the price
- ✗ Bad: Small text at the very bottom
- The guarantee removes the final objection. Don't hide it.

**Check 20: Is Scarcity/Urgency Real and Justified?**
- ✓ Good: "Cohort starts May 15th. Only 8 spots remaining" (real cohort limit)
- ✓ Good: "Early-bird price of $197 expires Sunday midnight" (real deadline)
- ✗ Bad: "Limited time offer" with no deadline
- ✗ Bad: Fake scarcity
- Fake scarcity kills trust. Use real constraints only.

**Check 21: Is the Pricing Framed as an Investment, Not a Cost?**
- ✓ Good: "Your investment of $497 pays for itself in 2 client closes"
- ✓ Good: "For the cost of one client close, you get..."
- ✗ Bad: "Only $97" (sounds cheap, not valuable)
- Frame price relative to value gained.

**Check 22: Is There a Clear Anchor Price?**
- ✓ Good: "Standard price is $997, but if you join this week, pay $297"
- ✓ Good: "This $200/month course is yours for one payment of $497"
- ✗ Bad: No anchor price given
- Anchor = reference price that makes offer price seem reasonable.

---

### Section 4: Social Proof (Prueba Social)
Proof is what converts skeptics into buyers.

**Check 23: Are Testimonials Specific (With Numbers and Results)?**
- ✓ Good: "I went from 0 to $40K in sales in 6 weeks. I used the email template from day one." — Sarah, Service Provider
- ✗ Bad: "This course changed my life! Highly recommend!" — John
- Specific testimonials are 10x more credible.

**Check 24: Do Testimonials Address the Main Objections?**
- If objection is "I don't have sales experience," get testimonial from beginner
- If objection is "Won't work for my niche," get testimonial from that niche
- ✓ Good: Diverse testimonials addressing different concerns
- ✗ Bad: All testimonials saying same thing

**Check 25: Are There At Least 3 Different Types of Proof?**
Types: testimonials, case studies, data/statistics, credentials, awards, media mentions, notable clients, before/after screenshots, usage numbers
- ✓ Good: 1 detailed case study + 3 client testimonials + quantified usage data
- ✗ Bad: Only testimonials
- Variety = higher trust

**Check 26: Are Results Attributed to Real People?**
- ✓ Good: "Jane Smith, Sales Manager at TechCorp, increased close rate to 60%"
- ✓ Good: Full name, headshot, company, specific result
- ✗ Bad: "Anonymous customer says..." OR initials only
- Real attribution = real results. Protect privacy but be specific about identity.

**Check 27: Is There "Herd Proof"?**
Herd proof = aggregated social proof. "X number of people have used this."
- ✓ Good: "10,000+ entrepreneurs have used this system"
- ✓ Good: "$50M in sales generated by our students"
- ✓ Good: "97% satisfaction rate across 5,000+ students"
- ✗ Bad: No herd proof
- People follow the crowd. Show them the crowd is big.

---

### Section 5: Copy Quality (Calidad de Redacción)
The mechanics that make copy readable and persuasive.

**Check 28: Is Every Sentence Under 15 Words?**
- Count: "People who read about the benefits of exercise often see immediate improvements in their mood and energy levels throughout the day." (24 words - TOO LONG)
- Rewrite: "Exercise improves mood fast. Energy surges within days." (8 words + 5 words = Better)
- Long sentences = reader fatigue = lower conversions

**Check 29: Is It Written in First/Second Person?**
- ✓ Good: "You'll discover the exact script I used..." OR "I made $50K with this system"
- ✓ Good: "Your first client closes in 30 days, guaranteed"
- ✗ Bad: "One can benefit from learning sales techniques" (passive, distant)
- ✗ Bad: "The system helps entrepreneurs increase revenue" (third person = low emotion)
- First/second person creates connection. Third person feels corporate.

**Check 30: Are There Zero Corporate Jargon Words?**
Corporate jargon to eliminate:
- "Leverage" → use, apply, employ
- "Synergy" → delete it (always)
- "Solution" → name what it actually is (system, template, method)
- "Innovative" → describe what makes it different
- "Comprehensive" → list what's included
- "Optimize" → improve, fix, increase
- "Paradigm shift" → change how you think about X
- "Touch point" → interaction, conversation, meeting
- "Ecosystem" → community, system, platform
- "Next-level" → better, faster, more effective

Test: Read it aloud. Would you say it to a friend? If not, change it.

**Check 31: Does Every Paragraph Move the Reader Forward?**
- ✓ Good: Each paragraph reveals new info or escalates desire
- ✗ Bad: Paragraph repeats what was already said
- ✗ Bad: Filler paragraphs with no purpose
- Ask for each paragraph: "If I deleted this, would the copy still work?" If yes, delete it.

**Check 32: Are Features Translated Into Benefits and Then Into Outcomes?**
Feature → Benefit → Outcome translation (see Section 3 for full details)
- Feature: "Video training library with 47 modules"
- Benefit: "Learn the exact system at your own pace"
- Outcome: "Master the skill in half the time it takes competitors"
- ✓ Good copy uses all three layers
- ✗ Bad copy stops at features ("We have a video library")

**Check 33: Does the Copy Read at an 8th-Grade Level?**
- Use short words (get, help, use vs. utilize, facilitate)
- Use short sentences (under 15 words)
- Use active voice (you get results, not results are obtained)
- Aim for Flesch Kincaid Grade 6-8
- Test: Paste copy into an online readability checker

**Check 34: Is There ONE Clear CTA (Call-to-Action)?**
- ✓ Good: One primary CTA that appears 3+ times
- ✗ Bad: "Join now OR buy now OR schedule a call OR email us"
- Rule: One action path. Everything else is noise.
- Place CTA at: top, middle, bottom of copy

**Check 35: Does the CTA Tell Them Exactly What to Do Next?**
- ✓ Good: "Click the button below to claim your spot" (specific action)
- ✓ Good: "Enter your email to get instant access" (what happens next)
- ✗ Bad: "Click here" (vague)
- ✗ Bad: "Learn more" (doesn't signal value)
- After CTA, reader should know exactly what happens next.

---

### Section 6: Emotional Architecture (La Arquitectura Emocional)
The journey from pain to desire.

**Check 36: Does It Start With the Reader's Current Pain?**
- ✓ Good: "Staring at your laptop at 11 PM, knowing you haven't made a single sale this week..."
- ✓ Good: "You've spent $20K on courses. Still no results."
- ✗ Bad: Jumping straight to the solution
- Don't solve immediately. Dwell in their pain first.

**Check 37: Does It Transition From Pain to Possibility?**
The emotional flow should be: pain → frustration → hope → momentum → peak desire
- ✓ Good: Paint pain → Show what's possible → Introduce your offer as the bridge
- ✗ Bad: Pain → immediate solution (feels forced)
- Transition questions: "What if...?" "Imagine if..." "The question isn't how hard it is, it's..."

**Check 38: Does It End on a Peak of Desire or Fear of Missing Out?**
- ✓ Good: Final sentences create peak desire or FOMO
- ✓ Good: "In 90 days, you could be the highest-paid person in your network"
- ✓ Good: "This cohort closes in 4 days. The next one isn't until June."
- ✗ Bad: Ending with logistics (shipping info, terms)
- Final emotion is strongest. Make it count.

**Check 39: Does It Use "You" More Than "We"?**
- Count occurrences of "you" vs. "we"
- ✓ Good: 70% "you" / 30% "we"
- ✗ Bad: 70% "we" / 30% "you"
- The reader cares about themselves, not about you. Use "you."

**Check 40: Would Someone Who Doesn't Buy Feel Like They're Leaving Money on the Table?**
- ✓ Good: Non-buyer thinks, "Damn, I might've made a mistake"
- ✗ Bad: Non-buyer thinks, "Okay, maybe I didn't need it anyway"
- The gap between outcomes promised and not getting them should feel painful.

---

### Scoring System (Puntuación)

| Score | Interpretation | Action |
|-------|---|---|
| 35-40 | Ready to publish | Launch immediately |
| 28-34 | Minor tweaks needed | Fix weak sections, re-test CTR |
| 20-27 | Significant rewrite required | Rewrite all weak sections, get feedback |
| Below 20 | Start over | Begin with core frameworks (Sections 2-4) |

**How to Score:**
- Give 1 point for fully meeting each check
- Give 0.5 points for partially meeting
- Give 0 points for missing completely
- Total possible: 40 points

## 2. Hormozi Voice Rules (Reglas de Voz Hormozi)
### The Specific Writing Style That Makes Hormozi Copy Work

Alex Hormozi's voice is distinctive and replicable. Follow these rules consistently across all copy.

---

### Tone Characteristics

#### 1. Direct
**Rule**: Say what you mean. No hedging, no "might," no "could potentially."
- ✓ Good: "This will triple your sales"
- ✗ Bad: "This could potentially help you increase sales in some cases"
- ✗ Bad: "Might help most people get better results"
- When you hedge, readers trust less. Confidence compounds credibility.

#### 2. Confident
**Rule**: Speak from authority. No "I think" or "in my opinion."
- ✓ Good: "After coaching 500+ business owners, I know what works"
- ✓ Good: "The research is clear: this method converts 40% higher"
- ✗ Bad: "I think this might be the best approach"
- ✗ Bad: "In my opinion, you should probably try..."
- Opinion sounds weak. Authority sounds credible.

#### 3. Specific
**Rule**: Numbers, timeframes, exact results. Never vague.
- ✓ Good: "Go from $0-$10K in 90 days" or "Save 8 hours per week"
- ✗ Bad: "Get better results faster" or "Save significant time"
- ✗ Bad: "Most people see improvement pretty quickly"
- Vague claims aren't credible. Specific claims are memorable.

#### 4. Conversational
**Rule**: Write like you talk. Read it aloud — if it sounds weird, rewrite.
- ✓ Good: "Here's what you need to know" / "This is where most people mess up"
- ✗ Bad: "Herein lies the fundamental principle of establishing optimal client acquisition"
- Test: Read your copy aloud. Does it sound like natural speech or a textbook?
- Conversational = relatable. Academic = distant.

#### 5. Provocative
**Rule**: Challenge the reader's current beliefs. Make them uncomfortable.
- ✓ Good: "Everything you learned about sales is wrong. Here's why:"
- ✓ Good: "Your current approach is leaving $100K on the table"
- ✗ Bad: "Consider trying a different sales methodology"
- Provocation creates attention. Niceness creates scrolls.
- Note: Provocation should be backed up by evidence immediately.

#### 6. Empathetic
**Rule**: Show you understand their pain BEFORE offering solutions.
- ✓ Good: "You've been grinding for 3 years, barely breaking $50K. You're tired. And you're starting to wonder if you're cut out for this."
- ✗ Bad: "Join our program and start earning more money!"
- Empathy builds trust. Trust leads to conversion.
- Spend 30-50% of copy on pain acknowledgment before mentioning solution.

---

### Sentence Construction Rules (Reglas de Construcción)

#### Maximum 15 Words Per Sentence
- ✓ Good (11 words): "Most people fail because they focus on the wrong metric."
- ✓ Good (7 words): "Here's what changes everything."
- ✗ Bad (23 words): "The primary reason that most entrepreneurs fail to achieve their financial goals is that they fail to understand the fundamental principles of value creation."
- Long sentences = reader fatigue. Short sentences = clarity.

#### One Idea Per Sentence
- ✓ Good: "Close rates triple when you use this script. Here's why." (2 sentences, 2 ideas)
- ✗ Bad: "Close rates triple when you use this script because it addresses the core objections and shows social proof while also creating urgency." (1 sentence, multiple ideas)
- Readers can only process one idea at a time. One idea per sentence = easier comprehension.

#### Start Sentences With Action Verbs When Possible
- ✓ Good: "Close the sale in the discovery call." / "Eliminate objections before they start." / "Stack proof to build credibility."
- ✗ Bad: "The most important thing is closing the sale." / "It's critical to eliminate objections."
- Action verbs = energy. Weak verbs = boredom.
- Action verbs: get, grab, claim, discover, unlock, eliminate, smash, dominate, master, execute

#### Use Fragments for Emphasis
Fragments are incomplete sentences. They work for punch.
- ✓ Good: "Three months later, she landed a $50K client. Without cold calling. Without paid ads. Without connections. Just the system."
- ✓ Good: "People fail because they quit. At the wrong time. After 90 days. When results were 30 days away."
- Impact. That's what fragments do.
- Rule: Use fragments sparingly (5-10% of copy). Overuse dilutes impact.

#### Alternate Between Short and Medium Sentences
- Short: 3-5 words. "Get access now."
- Medium: 8-12 words. "Join 5,000 entrepreneurs who doubled their revenue."
- Long: Up to 15 words. "The method has been tested on 500+ business owners across 10 industries."
- Pattern: short + medium + medium + short. Creates rhythm. Rhythm = readability.

#### Never Start 2 Consecutive Sentences the Same Way
- ✗ Bad: "You've tried everything. You've hired coaches. You've bought courses. You've read books."
- ✓ Better: "You've tried everything. Every coach says something different. Every course costs more. Every book makes different promises."
- Varied sentence starters = less repetitive = more engaging.
- Common starters to rotate: This is why, Here's the thing, The reality is, What most people miss, The difference between, Consider this

---

### Paragraph Rules

#### Maximum 3 Sentences Per Paragraph in Sales Copy
- ✓ Good:
  ```
  You've tried everything to close more sales.
  Nothing worked.
  Here's why — and what actually does work.
  ```
- ✗ Bad: 5-7 sentences per paragraph (walls of text)
- Short paragraphs = more white space = easier on eyes = higher read-through rate

#### Maximum 4 Lines Visually (On Mobile)
- Even a 3-sentence paragraph might be 4+ lines on mobile
- Test: View your copy on iPhone
- If paragraph is 5+ lines, break it up
- Mobile is 80% of reads. Optimize for mobile.

#### Each Paragraph Should Make the Reader Want to Read the Next One
- ✓ Good paragraph ending: "Here's where most people mess up..." (creates curiosity)
- ✓ Good paragraph ending: "But there's one thing nobody talks about." (sets up next reveal)
- ✗ Bad paragraph ending: "That's why this course is helpful." (conclusion, not a hook)
- Every paragraph should have a hook at the end. The hook is the bridge to next paragraph.

#### End Paragraphs With a Hook, Not a Conclusion
- ✗ Bad hook: "And that's why understanding sales is important."
- ✓ Good hook: "But here's the one thing that changes everything:"
- ✓ Good hook: "That's when she discovered the real problem."
- ✓ Good hook: "And that's when everything shifted."
- Hooks create forward momentum. Conclusions create stopping points.

---

### Power Words (Categorized by Function)

#### Urgency Power Words
Use when you need to create FOMO or deadline effect.
- now, today, immediately, fast, instant, deadline, limited, closing soon, ending, expires, urgent, don't wait, final chance, last spots

**Example use**: "Cohort closes TODAY. Only 3 spots left."

#### Exclusivity Power Words
Use when you want to make someone feel special/selected.
- only, selected, invitation, private, inner circle, elite, founding, VIP, limited access, hand-picked, by-invitation-only, rare, exclusive

**Example use**: "You've been selected for early access."

#### Value Power Words
Use to highlight what they get/save.
- free, bonus, included, extra, guaranteed, proven, tested, certified, verified, done-for-you, ready-to-use, plug-and-play

**Example use**: "Everything is done-for-you. Just plug it in."

#### Emotion Power Words
Use to create feeling/desire.
- finally, imagine, discover, secret, revealed, breakthrough, transform, unlock, master, dominate, conquer, crush, breakthrough, epiphany, aha

**Example use**: "Finally, you can stop wondering if you're cut out for this."

#### Specificity Power Words
Use when you want to feel precise/detailed.
- exactly, step-by-step, word-for-word, blueprint, system, formula, method, framework, playbook, script, template, process, mechanism

**Example use**: "The exact 3-step formula we use with all our clients."

## 3. Feature → Benefit → Outcome Translation (Traducción FBO)
### The 3-Step Process to Turn Boring Features Into Compelling Copy

This framework transforms bland product descriptions into emotionally resonant value propositions.

---

### The Framework Explained

**Feature**: What the product/service IS
- Objective, factual description
- What exists in the product
- What the customer receives

**Benefit**: What it DOES for them
- How it impacts their life/business immediately
- What becomes possible or easier
- What they can now do

**Outcome**: How their LIFE CHANGES
- The transformation that results from using the benefit repeatedly
- Who they become
- What's now possible at a deeper level
- The status/identity shift

---

### The Translation Process

**Step 1: List the Feature**
- Be specific about what you're offering
- Example: "Access to video training library with 47 modules"

**Step 2: Ask "So What?" - Find the Benefit**
- "So what does this DO for the customer?"
- Answer: "They can learn the system at their own pace without a coach yelling at them"
- Benefit: Learn at your own pace, reduce overwhelm, work around your schedule

**Step 3: Ask "So What?" Again - Find the Real Outcome**
- "So what if they learn at their own pace?"
- "Where does that lead?"
- Answer: "They master the skill without months of expensive 1-on-1 coaching. They're confident in their ability. They're ready to teach others."
- Outcome: Master the skill faster than peers, become the expert in your circle, never depend on coaching again

**Step 4: Combine Into Copy**
- Don't list all three. Weave them together
- Start with outcome (most compelling)
- Use feature and benefit as proof
- Example: "You'll master this in 90 days (Outcome) through video training at your own pace (Benefit) because we've pre-recorded all 47 modules (Feature) you'd normally spend 6 months learning."

### The "So What?" Test
**The Most Important Copywriting Test**

When you've written something, ask yourself "So what?" Keep asking until you reach the outcome.

**Example:**
- Feature: "This course has video training"
- **So what?** (Benefit) You can learn without live classes
- **So what?** (Outcome) You get the knowledge without the time commitment
- **So what?** (Deeper outcome) You're finally ready to sell
- **So what?** (Life change) You quit your job and start your business

The deeper you go with "so what," the more compelling your copy becomes. The outcome (life change) is always more powerful than the feature (video training).

---

### Template for Feature → Benefit → Outcome Copy

Use this structure when writing any product description or sales page section:

```
[Outcome statement - most powerful]
How? [Feature details - credibility]
Because [Benefit - the mechanism that creates outcome]
Your result: [Specific transformation]
```

**Example:**
"Master sales in 90 days without a coach (Outcome) through our pre-recorded video system (Feature) that breaks down the exact conversations top closers use (Benefit). Your result: Close your first high-ticket client and prove to yourself you can do this."

## 4. Before/After Transformation Framework (Marco Antes/Después)
### How to Paint the Transformation Your Offer Creates

The Before/After framework is the emotional core of every sales page. It shows the gap your offer bridges.

---

### The Before State (El Estado Antes - Pain)

Your goal is to paint the reader's current life in vivid, specific terms. Make them FEEL the pain.

**Questions to Answer:**
1. **What does their day look like?**
   - Time: What time do they wake up? What are they thinking about?
   - Emotion: How do they feel? Frustrated? Anxious? Hopeless?
   - Action: What do they do first? Check email? Panic?
   - Example: "5 AM. You're awake. Again. Thinking about that deal that fell apart yesterday. You already know today will be the same cycle."

2. **What keeps them awake at 3 AM?**
   - The specific fear, concern, or worry
   - Example: "Will I ever close a deal? Did I say something wrong? Are my competitors better than me?"
   - This is deeper than surface pain

3. **What have they tried that failed?**
   - Previous attempts, investments, pivots
   - Make reader nod: "Yes, I've tried that"
   - Example: "You've hired coaches. Bought courses. Watched YouTube. Tried scripts. Nothing stuck."

4. **What do they secretly believe about themselves?**
   - The limiting belief underneath the pain
   - Example: "Maybe I'm just not a natural salesman. Maybe some people can do this and some can't."
   - This is where deepest conversion happens

5. **What's the cost of NOT solving this?**
   - Economic: "$100K less per year"
   - Emotional: "Another year of wondering if you're cut out for this"
   - Timeline: "By next year, your friend will have built a business and you'll still be stuck"
   - Example: "Keep going like this and in 5 years you'll have made $50K total. Your friend will have made $500K. And you'll still be wondering why sales is so hard for everyone else."

---

### The After State (El Estado Después - Paradise)

Paint the opposite of pain. Make them CRAVE this transformation.

**Questions to Answer:**
1. **What does a perfect day look like?**
   - Time: When do they wake? How do they feel?
   - Action: What's different about their day?
   - Environment: Where are they? Who's around?
   - Example: "8 AM. You wake up refreshed. You check your email and there's a new high-ticket client application waiting. Another one. By Thursday you'll close $10K. Just like last week."

2. **What results have they achieved?**
   - Specific numbers, timelines, metrics
   - Example: "You've closed 8 clients this month. $80K in revenue. More than you made all of last year. In 4 weeks."
   - Make numbers SPECIFIC and BIG

3. **How do they feel about themselves?**
   - Confidence, pride, identity shift
   - Example: "You're not wondering anymore. You know you're good at this. You're one of the top 1% of closers in your industry."
   - The IDENTITY change is crucial

4. **What do other people say about them?**
   - Reputation, status, respect
   - Example: "People ask for your advice. You're the 'sales person.' When someone needs help closing, they call you first."
   - This is the external validation of the internal change

5. **What's now possible that wasn't before?**
   - Freedom, choices, opportunities
   - Example: "You could choose to work with only $50K clients now. Or scale to 5 clients at $20K each. Or take summers off. The point is you're choosing, not desperate."
   - Show the expanded horizon

---

## 5. Objection Handling in Copy (Manejo de Objeciones)
### The 7 Universal Objections and How to Pre-Handle Them

Objections kill conversions. Pre-handle them in your copy BEFORE the reader thinks them.

---

### Objection 1: "Too Expensive" (Es Muy Caro)

**Why it happens**: Reader calculates price without calculating value gained or cost of inaction.

**Pre-handling Strategies:**

**Strategy A: Reframe as Cost of Inaction**
```
You might think $497 is expensive.
But here's what's actually expensive:
Spending another year at $50K revenue.
Making $400K less than your peers.
Still being dependent on 1-on-1 coaching at $250/hour.
$497 is a bargain compared to what you're actually spending.
```

**Strategy B: Daily Cost Breakdown**
```
$497 for 90 days of access.
That's $5.50 per day.
Less than a coffee.
You'll make back the investment by day 2 of the training.
```

**Strategy C: ROI Comparison**
```
Your investment: $497
Typical result: First high-ticket client worth $5K-$10K
ROI: 1,000% in the first 30 days
Compare that to the stock market (8-10% annual) or any course ($2K-$5K with 0% ROI).
```

**Strategy D: Value Stack (show what they're getting)**
```
Video Training: $497
Email Templates: $297
Private Community: $997
30-Day Guarantee: Priceless
Done-for-You Scripts: $197
Bonus: Implementation Roadmap ($197)

Total value: $2,185
Your investment: $497
You save: $1,688
```

---

### Objection 2: "Won't Work for Me" (No Funcionará para Mí)

**Why it happens**: Reader sees themselves as different/unique/harder case than proof examples.

**Pre-handling Strategies:**

**Strategy A: Testimonials From "Different" People**
- If you serve multiple niches, show that
- "From freelancers to agencies to consultants — all got results"
- Include testimonials from the reader's specific situation
- Variation proves universality

**Strategy B: The "Even If" Statement**
```
Even if:
- You've tried sales training before (and failed)
- You're introverted and uncomfortable selling
- You don't have a network
- You're in a competitive market
- You're starting from zero

You'll still get results because this system works through [mechanism], not through [myth they believed].
```

**Strategy C: Explain Why It Works**
```
Why this works when other things didn't:
Previous training taught you features and benefits.
This teaches you the psychology of the buyer.
That's the difference.
Psychology works regardless of your personality.
```

---

### Objection 3: "Don't Have Time" (No Tengo Tiempo)

**Why it happens**: Reader is busy and sees the program as requiring lots of time.

**Pre-handling Strategies:**

**Strategy A: Time Efficiency Claims**
```
Most programs demand 10+ hours per week.
This demands 30 minutes per day.
That's video consumption during your commute.
Implementation happens in between client calls.
By week 3, new habits save you time overall.
Net result: You're busier but making 3x more money.
```

**Strategy B: Guarantee You Can Do It While Working**
```
This is designed for working professionals.
100% of our most successful students stayed employed while learning.
You're not choosing between this and your current income.
You do both, then transition.
```

**Strategy C: Practical Time Breakdown**
```
Week 1: Watch 4 videos (30 min each) = 2 hours
Week 2: Implement scripts in 2 calls = 1 hour (built into work day)
Week 3: Adjust based on results = 30 minutes

Most of the "work" is done in your existing client calls.
You're not adding time. You're redirecting it.
```

---

### Objection 4: "I've Tried Before" (Ya Intenté Antes)

**Why it happens**: Reader tried something similar and failed. They believe they're incapable.

**Pre-handling Strategies:**

**Strategy A: Acknowledge and Explain Why**
```
You've tried sales training.
It didn't work.
Here's why: Most programs teach you what to say.
They never teach you the MECHANISM.
You learn words without understanding psychology.
Words without psychology = no closes.

This time is different because you'll learn the mechanism.
```

**Strategy B: Mechanism-Focused Proof**
```
Previous attempt: You learned techniques
This approach: You learn why techniques work
That's the difference between memorizing and understanding.
Understanding sticks. Techniques fade.
```

**Strategy C: Show What Was Missing**
```
What you tried before was missing:
- Specific scripts for your exact situation
- Live feedback on your execution
- Community of people doing the same thing
- Daily accountability

We've added all of those.
Here's the proof it works with them: [testimonial]
```

---

### Objection 5: "I Need to Think About It" (Necesito Pensarlo)

**Why it happens**: Reader is on the fence. Without urgency, they'll forget and never buy.

**Pre-handling Strategies:**

**Strategy A: Create Real Urgency**
```
This cohort closes Friday.
We open a new one in 8 weeks.
The difference between joining Friday vs. June 1st?
6 weeks of your competition ahead of you.
In this market, that's the difference between thriving and surviving.
```

**Strategy B: Reframe "Thinking" as Procrastination**
```
You don't need to think about this more.
You already know:
- You're stuck at your current revenue
- You've tried to fix it
- Nothing has worked alone
- Someone else figured it out

The thinking was done months ago.
Now it's execution time.
```

**Strategy C: What They Lose by Waiting**
```
Every day you don't implement is a day someone else is closing deals with your future clients.
By next month, your competitor will be 30 days ahead.
By year end, they'll have made $200K more than you.
That's what "thinking about it" costs.
```

---

### Objection 6: "Not the Right Time" (No Es el Momento Correcto)

**Why it happens**: Reader creates a hypothetical future when they'll be "ready" that never comes.

**Pre-handling Strategies:**

**Strategy A: The Right Time Reframe**
```
When will be the right time?
When you're less busy? [You won't be]
When you have more money? [You will, after this]
When you're more confident? [You get confident by doing]

The right time is now.
Not because the timing is perfect.
Because the timing is never perfect.
But starting today beats waiting forever.
```

**Strategy B: Show What "Waiting" Costs**
```
I get it. You want to wait until:
- Business is slower (it won't be)
- You've saved more money (you'll spend it elsewhere)
- You're more experienced (you'll plateau without this)

The hidden cost of waiting: Opportunity cost.
Every month you wait, your future clients are being sold by someone else.
By the time you're "ready," the market has moved.
Start now, get ready while doing.
```

**Strategy C: Identify the Real Objection**
```
When people say "not the right time," they usually mean one of these:
1. I'm scared this won't work [Handled by guarantee]
2. I'm scared I'll fail [Handled by community + accountability]
3. I don't have the money [We offer payment plans]
4. Something else is holding me back [Reply to this email, let's talk]

What's actually holding you back?
```

---

### Objection 7: "I Don't Trust This" (No Confío en Esto)

**Why it happens**: Reader has been burned before or skeptical by nature.

**Pre-handling Strategies:**

**Strategy A: Stack Multiple Proof Types**
```
You have reasons to be skeptical.
Here's why you can trust this:

1. 5,000+ entrepreneurs have used this system [Herd proof]
2. $50M generated by our students [Aggregate proof]
3. [Specific person] increased revenue from $80K to $240K in 90 days [Specific proof]
4. Here's exactly how it works [Mechanism proof]
5. 30-day money-back guarantee [Risk elimination]
6. I've built a $10M business using these exact principles [Credibility proof]

Choose one proof that matters most to you.
```

**Strategy B: Guarantee is the Ultimate Trust Signal**
```
I'm confident enough to put my money where my mouth is.
30-day money-back guarantee.
Get full access. Implement for a month.
If you don't see results, you get every dollar back.
No questions asked.
I'm not offering this because I have to.
I'm offering it because I've never had someone ask for it back.
```

**Strategy C: Transparency About Limitations**
```
This isn't for everyone.
It won't work if:
- You're not willing to practice the scripts
- You're not willing to make calls
- You expect it to work without implementation
- You're looking for a passive income tool

It WILL work if:
- You're willing to implement
- You want to go from $0-$10K clients
- You want to avoid hiring a $5K/month coach
- You're ready to be the expert in your market
```

## 6. Copy Formulas Quick Reference (Fórmulas de Copy)
### 10 Copy Formulas for Any Situation

Use these pre-built structures to accelerate your copy writing. Each has specific use cases and strengths.

---

### Formula 1: PAS (Problem-Agitate-Solve)
**Best for**: Sales emails, landing pages, ads
**Structure**:
```
PROBLEM: State the reader's problem specifically
[1-2 sentences painting their pain]

AGITATE: Make it worse. Show the cost of staying stuck.
[2-3 sentences that escalate the pain]

SOLVE: Introduce your solution
[1-2 sentences about how your offer solves it]

CTA: What's next?
```

**Example**:
```
Problem: You've been trying to close high-ticket clients for 8 months.
Agitate: Meanwhile, your friend closed 5 this quarter and now charges 3x more than you.
Solve: There's a system the top 1% of closers use that actually works.
CTA: Click below to see the system.
```

---

### Formula 2: AIDA (Attention-Interest-Desire-Action)
**Best for**: Long-form sales pages, webinar scripts
**Structure**:
```
ATTENTION: Hook that stops the scroll
[Under 10 words, specific number or contradiction]

INTEREST: Explain why this matters
[What's at stake? Why should they care?]

DESIRE: Paint the transformation
[Paint the after state. Paint it vividly.]

ACTION: Clear CTA
[Exactly what they do next]
```

**Example**:
```
Attention: "How to close 40% more sales without hiring"
Interest: "Most closers hire assistants and lose 60% of deals. Here's why, and what works instead."
Desire: "[Paint the after state of consistent, high-ticket closes]"
Action: "Click the button below to see the 3-step system."
```

---

### Formula 3: BAB (Before-After-Bridge)
**Best for**: Product pages, testimonial pages, transformation stories
**Structure**:
```
BEFORE: Paint the pain state
[Vivid, specific detail about their current situation]

AFTER: Paint the transformed state
[Vivid, specific detail about life after solution]

BRIDGE: How they got from Before to After
[What's the missing piece? Your offer.]
```

**Example**:
```
Before: "Staring at your Stripe account at 11 PM. $2,000 this month. Another failed launch."
After: "Checking Stripe Monday morning: $15K in sales over the weekend. Your system worked."
Bridge: "The difference wasn't talent. It was having the right framework to close without discounting."
```

---

### Formula 4: The 4 U's (Urgent, Unique, Ultra-specific, Useful)
**Best for**: Subject lines, headlines, ad copy
**Structure**:
Create a headline that hits all 4:
- Urgent: Why now?
- Unique: Why this vs. alternatives?
- Ultra-specific: What exactly?
- Useful: What do they get?

**Formula**: [Specific number/metric] [result] in [timeframe] [without something/with this method]

**Examples**:
- "Close 3 more clients per month without hiring sales staff" (all 4 present)
- "7-figure revenue without a team" (unique + useful + specific)
- "The one script that closes 60% of cold calls" (specific + unique + useful)

---

### Formula 5: The 1-2-3-4 Formula
**Best for**: Welcome emails, intro copy, cold outreach
**Structure**:
```
1: What I've got (The offer)
2: What it'll do (The transformation)
3: Who am I (Credibility)
4: What next (CTA)
```

**Example Email**:
```
Subject: One script = 40% close rate

Hey [Name],

1. I've got a $5K/client closing script that actually works
2. Use it on your next 3 calls and close at least 1 (even if you've never sold before)
3. I've closed over $8M using this exact approach
4. Reply and I'll send you the script

Best,
[Your name]
```

---

### Formula 6: QUEST (Qualify-Understand-Educate-Stimulate-Transition)
**Best for**: Long sales funnels, webinar scripts, high-ticket offers
**Structure**:
```
QUALIFY: Who is this for? Who is it NOT for?
[Be extremely specific about who should pay attention]

UNDERSTAND: Show you deeply understand their situation
[Paint their pain in vivid detail]

EDUCATE: Teach something valuable for free
[Give real frameworks, actionable insight]

STIMULATE: Show what becomes possible
[Paint the transformation that education enables]

TRANSITION: Bridge from education to offer
[How your program accelerates what they learned]
```

**Best length**: 15-20 minute webinar, 3,000+ word sales page

---

### Formula 7: SLAP (Stop-Look-Act-Purchase)
**Best for**: Ads, emails, social media posts
**Structure**:
```
STOP: Hook so strong they pause their scroll
[Problem stated as contradiction or curiosity]

LOOK: Make them look deeper
[Hint at a mechanism or secret]

ACT: Tell them the action to take
[Specific action, one action only]

PURCHASE: Make the ask
[Price, offer, urgency]
```

**Example**:
```
Stop: "Most people try to close high-ticket deals exactly wrong"
Look: "They focus on features when they should focus on fear"
Act: "Here's the script top closers use:"
Purchase: "Get it for $97, or $7/month in our community"
```

---

### Formula 8: The 6+1 Formula (Context-Attention-Desire-Gap-Solution-CTA + Credibility)
**Best for**: Landing pages, sales pages, long-form emails
**Structure**:
```
CONTEXT: Where does this problem live?
[Set the scene, time period, market shift]

ATTENTION: Hook statement
[Specific claim about a result or solution]

DESIRE: Paint the transformation
[What becomes possible? How does life change?]

GAP: Show the gap between current and desired state
[Make the problem real, make the cost of inaction clear]

SOLUTION: Here's how to bridge the gap
[Your offer, explained as a system/framework]

CTA: What's next?
[One specific action]

+ CREDIBILITY: Who are you?
[Why should they trust you?]
```

---

### Formula 9: Star-Chain-Hook
**Best for**: Story-driven sales pages, YouTube videos, podcast pitches
**Structure**:
```
STAR: Introduce a character (could be you)
[Make them relatable, put them in a situation]

CHAIN: Event-by-event sequence that builds
[Keep pulling the thread, each sentence reveals more]

HOOK: The surprising truth or realization
[Unexpected twist, new perspective, lesson]
```

**Example Story**:
```
Star: "I was making $30K/year selling on my own."
Chain: "I hired a coach. $3K/month. She showed me one script.
I used it that week. Close rate went from 20% to 60%.
I realized it wasn't my personality. It was the mechanism."
Hook: "Every top closer knows this one thing. Most people never learn it."
```

---

### Formula 10: The Hormozi Direct (Hook-Proof-Offer-Guarantee-CTA)
**Best for**: Sales pages, videos, long-form content from Hormozi himself
**Structure**:
```
HOOK: The most compelling claim you can make
[Specific number, clear transformation, 1-2 sentences]

PROOF: Multiple types of proof stacked
[Testimonials, case studies, mechanism, data, credentials]

OFFER: Crystal clear what they get
[List all components with perceived values]

GUARANTEE: Risk reversal
[30-90 day guarantee, no questions asked, specific terms]

CTA: Single, clear action
[One button, one path, crystal clear what happens next]
```

**Example**:
```

Proof: [4-5 different proof types]
Offer: "You're getting: Video training + Scripts + Community + 1-on-1 calls"
Value stack shows total $2,000+, your price $297
Guarantee: "90-day money-back guarantee. If you don't see results, full refund."
CTA: "Claim your spot now — only 5 remaining"
```

## 7. Mobile-First Copy Rules (Reglas de Copy Mobile)
### Optimizing for 80%+ Reads on Mobile Devices

If you're not optimizing for mobile, you're losing 80% of conversions.

---

### Core Mobile Principles

**Principle 1: One Idea Per Screen Scroll**
- On desktop, reader sees 3-4 paragraphs at once
- On mobile, reader sees 5-8 sentences max
- Each screen should introduce ONE new idea
- Long-form sales pages need more white space on mobile

**Principle 2: Shorter Lines, More Whitespace**
- Desktop: 6-8 words per line is fine
- Mobile: 3-4 words per line is optimal
- Mobile copy looks longer when it's the same word count as desktop
- Embrace the length. It looks readable.

**Principle 3: Bold Key Phrases (Frases Clave)**
- Readers skim on mobile
- Bold the most important phrase in each paragraph
- Bold power words
- Bold numbers and metrics
- **Example**: "You'll close **3x more deals** in the first **90 days**"

**Principle 4: Short Paragraphs**
- Desktop: 3-4 sentences per paragraph is fine
- Mobile: 1-2 sentences per paragraph is better
- Breaks up visual weight
- Easier to skim

**Principle 5: Break Up Text With Elements**
Use these to create visual breaks:
- Numbered lists
- Bullet points
- Line breaks (whitespace)
- Bolded text
- Subheadings
- Short quotes

**Principle 6: CTAs Stay Visible**
- CTA button should never require more than 2 scrolls to reach
- Include CTA multiple times down the page
- Mobile users will close the app if CTA is buried
- Make buttons big (minimum 44x44 pixels for touch)

**Principle 7: Images Are Essential**
- Mobile readers trust images more than text
- Every section should have supporting visual
- Images break up text-heavy pages
- Testimonials need faces (photos)
- Case studies need screenshots

---

### Mobile Copy Template

Here's the optimal structure for mobile-first sales pages:

```
[SECTION 1: HOOK]
Headline (8-10 words, bold)
Subheading (2-3 words, smaller font)

[Visual: Hero image]

[SECTION 2: PAIN]
What's wrong with the current approach?
One sentence per paragraph.
Make them nod.

[Visual: Problem illustration]

[SECTION 3: PROOF]
Short testimonial (1-2 sentences)
Specific result (number)

Another testimonial

[SECTION 4: HOW]
Here's how to bridge the gap.
One sentence per point.

[Visual: System diagram]

[SECTION 5: OFFER]
What they get
Value stacking
Price

[CTA BUTTON - BIG]

[SECTION 6: GUARANTEE]
Risk reversal statement
What happens if it doesn't work

[SECTION 7: MORE PROOF]
Testimonials (mobile: show 1-2, tap to see more)

[CTA BUTTON - BIG]

[SECTION 8: FAQ]
Expandable Q&A format (click to reveal answer)

[CTA BUTTON - BIG]
```

---

### Mobile-Specific Formatting Rules

**Headline Rules for Mobile**:
- Maximum 8-10 words
- Break into 2-3 lines
- Use short, punchy words
- Never hyphenate words

**Paragraph Rules for Mobile**:
- Maximum 2-3 lines per paragraph
- Use line breaks liberally
- One idea per paragraph
- Always use subheadings between sections

**List Rules for Mobile**:
- Use bullets or numbers
- One item per line
- Max 5 items per list
- Separate lists with space

**Button Rules for Mobile**:
- Minimum 44x44 pixels (touch target)
- High contrast colors
- Text should say what happens: "Claim access" not "Click here"
- Repeat CTA every 2-3 scrolls

---

### Mobile Copy Do's and Don'ts

| DO | DON'T |
|---|---|
| Use shorter sentences (under 12 words) | Write long paragraphs (5+ sentences) |
| Bold important phrases | Use italics for emphasis (hard to read mobile) |
| Use numbered or bulleted lists | Use paragraph form for multiple points |
| Break long content with subheadings | Have 4+ paragraphs without a visual break |
| Repeat CTA multiple times | Put CTA only at bottom |
| Use high-contrast colors | Use light gray text on white background |
| Make buttons big (44x44px minimum) | Use small buttons or links |
| Use responsive images | Use images that don't adapt to screen size |
| Optimize for thumb reach (lower half of screen) | Put important elements at top |
| Test on actual mobile devices | Test only on browser zoom |
