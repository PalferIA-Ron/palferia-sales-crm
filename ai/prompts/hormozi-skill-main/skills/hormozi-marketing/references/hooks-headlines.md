## 1. THE 7-TYPE HOOK ARSENAL

### Type 1: The Contrarian Hook
**The Psychology:** Creates cognitive dissonance by contradicting deeply held beliefs. Forces the reader to pause and reconsider. The brain detects the challenge and demands resolution.

**Formula:** `"[Common belief] is wrong. Here's what actually [result]."`

**Why It Works:**
- Contradicts without attacking (avoids defensive response)
- Creates curiosity gap that MUST be closed
- Positions you as the authority who knows the real truth
- Loss aversion principle: people fear being wrong more than being validated

**Industry: Software/Tech**
1. "Automation doesn't save time. It saves the wrong thing."
2. "The more features you add, the fewer customers you attract."
3. "Your tech stack is why you're broke, not why you're fast."
4. "Microservices architecture destroys company growth."
5. "The best code is code that doesn't exist."
6. "Your API is too complex because your product is too simple."
7. "Scaling requires going slower, not faster."
8. "The more agile you claim to be, the less you probably are."
9. "Your CI/CD pipeline is running you into the ground."
10. "Security by obscurity actually works (when done right)."

**Industry: Fitness**
1. "You're not overweight because you eat too much. You're overweight because you eat the wrong things."
2. "Six-pack abs are 80% environment, not 80% diet."
3. "Running doesn't build endurance; it destroys it."
4. "The heavier you lift, the easier dieting becomes."
5. "Cardio is holding you back from real fitness."
6. "Your metabolism isn't broken; your meal timing is."
7. "Rest days don't help you recover; they make you weaker."
8. "Stretching before workouts actually increases injury risk."
9. "Calories don't matter nearly as much as your hormones."
10. "The gym is not where you build muscle. Sleep is."

**Industry: Business/Startups**
1. "Raising capital destroys 90% of startups that do it."
2. "Your business plan is fantasy until it survives the market."
3. "Hiring fast is exactly how you fail fast."
4. "Scaling before you've found product-market fit is suicide."
5. "Hustle culture is expensive debt you'll pay forever."
6. "Your competition is not who you think it is."
7. "Market size is the worst metric to evaluate your business."
8. "Your MVP is too big to be minimum."
9. "Growth hacking is code for 'we don't have a real product.'"
10. "The best businesses grow by saying no, not yes."

**When to Use:**
- Thought leadership positioning

## 2. PATTERN INTERRUPT SYSTEM: The Science of Stopping the Scroll

Pattern interrupts work because humans are literally built to notice what breaks the pattern. When everything looks the same, we ignore it. The first thing different gets all the attention.

### VISUAL PATTERN INTERRUPTS

**Movement in First Frame**
- The eye is hardwired to detect motion (evolutionary survival mechanism)
- In a static feed, 1 second of movement = 500% more stops
- Effective for: Video ads, carousel posts, animated graphics
- Template: Start with 0.5-1 second of movement before message appears

**Text Overlay with Contrasting Colors**
- Color contrast stops the eye before messaging is read
- Neon/bright colors on dark backgrounds = highest attention
- Effective for: Image posts, video subtitles, thumbnails
- Template: Use 1-2 colors max; contrast should be "why is that bright yellow there?"

**Unexpected Imagery**
- The brain expects congruence between message and image
- Incongruence forces resolution = attention spike
- Effective for: Social media posts, email headers, ads
- Template: Image that seems to contradict the headline (creates curiosity gap)

**Face Close-Ups with Strong Emotion**
- Faces get processed in 200ms (faster than text)
- Emotion in faces is contagious and compelling
- Effective for: Ads, email headers, video thumbnails
- Template: Close-up face (not cheesy, authentic) showing specific emotion related to result

**Blank Space with Minimal Elements**
- Creates "psychological breathing room"
- Forces reader to fill in details (more engagement)
- Effective for: Sales pages, premium offers, high-ticket ads
- Template: 80% white space, 20% strategic elements

### VERBAL PATTERN INTERRUPTS

**Start Mid-Story (No Introduction)**
- Conventional content starts with "here's the problem..."
- Pattern interrupt: Jump to climax, back up to explain
- Example: "I woke up to a text from my accountant: 'You made $847K this month.' 3 years prior, I was broke."
- Effective because: Doesn't feel like marketing, feels like conversation

**Open with the Result, Not the Setup**
- Normal copy: Problem → Solution → Result
- Pattern interrupt: Result → Why it's possible → How to get it
- Example: "$23K in new revenue last month using just email" (vs. "We created an email system...")
- Effective because: Shows you what's possible before convincing you it's possible

**Use a Shocking Statement in First 3 Words**
- First 3 words determine if someone reads the 4th
- Best openers: Numbers, "You," "Stop," "This," "What," "Why"
- Bad openers: "I," "So," "Hey," "In this," "The reason"
- Example Strong: "97% of businesses fail because..."
- Example Weak: "The reason most businesses fail is..."

**Ask a Question that Demands an Answer**
- Statements can be ignored; questions cannot
- The brain must answer before continuing
- Example: "How much money have you left on the table by not knowing this?"
- Effective because: Requires cognitive engagement

### THE FIRST 3 WORDS RULE

**Power Opening Words (Always Work)**
- Numbers: "473 people," "92% of," "$50K in," "Last year"
- "You": "You're probably," "You don't know," "You could be"
- "What": "What if," "What most," "What nobody's"
- "Why": "Why most," "Why your," "Why you don't"
- "This": "This changes," "This is," "This works because"
- "Stop": "Stop wasting," "Stop believing," "Stop paying"
- Question words: "How much," "When was," "Does this"

## 3. M-A-G-I-C NAMING FORMULA
### The Complete System for Naming Offers, Products, Programs & Lead Magnets

Naming is positioning. A good name does the selling for you. A bad name requires explanation.

**M = Make a Magnetic Reason Why**
Why does this exist? What moment, discovery, or event created it?
- The "why" becomes the story you tell
- Makes the offer feel inevitable ("of course he created this")
- Clients connect with the origin story more than the offer itself
- Best reason whys: Accident, obsession, problem solved for thousands, life-changing discovery

**Examples:**
- "The 2-Hour Workweek Blueprint" (Why: He wanted to stop working 80-hour weeks)
- "The $50K/Month Client Getting System" (Why: His student landed this exact deal using his method)
- "$100K One-Person Business Playbook" (Why: He built this and wanted to systematize what worked)

**A = Announce Your Avatar**
Who is this specifically for? What's their specific situation?
- Specific avatars convert 3x better than generic ones
- Use occupation, aspiration, or problem situation
- "For [Specific Person Who Has Specific Problem]"

**Examples:**
- "For Consultants Stuck at $100K"
- "For Burned-Out Dentists Ready to Own Their Practice"
- "For First-Time Founders with Traction but No Playbook"

**G = Give Them a Goal**
What specific outcome will they achieve?
- Goal should be measurable and specific
- Should feel achievable but not obvious
- Use either $ or time-based outcomes

**Examples:**
- "Generate $10K/Month"
- "Launch in 30 Days"
- "Add 50 Qualified Leads"
- "Escape Your Job by December"

**I = Indicate a Time Interval**
How fast? This creates urgency and feasibility.
- Intervals create belief (faster = more credible)
- Too long feels unrealistic; too short feels like luck
- Sweet spots: 30 days, 60 days, 90 days, 6 months, 1 year

**Examples:**
- "In 90 Days"
- "In 6 Weeks"
- "By Year-End"
- "Before Summer"

**C = Complete with a Container Word**
What IS this thing? What's the container/format?
- Container words are psychological—they create framework in the buyer's mind
- Different containers for different psychology
- The same content in a "challenge" vs. "system" vs. "mastermind" feels completely different

## 5. SUB-HEADLINES & SUPPORTING COPY
### The Job of the Sub-Headline

The sub-headline has three possible jobs:
1. **Specify who** (target the right person)
2. **Add credibility** (prove it's real)
3. **Create curiosity** (elaborate on the promise)

Most headlines need sub-headlines to work. Main headline gets them to stop; sub-headline gets them to read.

### 15 SUB-HEADLINE TEMPLATES

1. **The Avatar Specifier**
   - Formula: `"For [specific avatar] who want [specific outcome]."`
   - Example: "For consultants who've hit the $100K ceiling and want to break through"
   - Use when: Main headline is broad; you need to target precisely

2. **The Curiosity Expander**
   - Formula: `"[Hint at the specific mechanism without revealing it]."`
   - Example: "Without changing your audience, your offer, or your pricing"
   - Use when: Main headline is vague; you need to intrigue

3. **The Proof Statement**
   - Formula: `"[Credibility marker]: [specific result from specific people]."`

### HOW TO PAIR HEADLINES + SUB-HEADLINES FOR MAXIMUM IMPACT

**Pairing Rule 1: Main Headline Makes Promise; Sub-Headline Proves It**
- Main: "Go from $0-$100K in 12 Months"
- Sub: "5,847 entrepreneurs have using this exact system"
- Why: Claim + proof = conversion

**Pairing Rule 2: Main Headline Emotional; Sub-Headline Logical**
- Main: "Finally Own Your Time Again"

## 7. HOOK SCORING SYSTEM
### Evaluate Hooks Before Publishing

Use this scoring system to rate your hooks. A hook scoring 6+/10 is publishable. 8+/10 is high-performing.

### THE HOOK SCORE CARD

**Dimension 1: Specificity (1-10)**
How specific is your claim? Generic claims = low scores.
- 1-3: Generic ("Get better results," "Make more money," "Be happy")
- 4-6: Somewhat specific ("Make $50K," "Lose 30 pounds," "Get 100 leads")
- 7-10: Extremely specific ("Make $47,293 in 90 days," "Go from 0-3 six-figure clients," "Cut customer acquisition cost by 62%")
- Scoring: Count the specific numbers/timeframes. More = higher score.

**Dimension 2: Curiosity Gap (1-10)**
Does your hook create a gap between what they know and what they want to know?
- 1-3: Too obvious ("Here's how to make money," "Learn about marketing")
- 4-6: Moderate gap ("Why most businesses fail," "The secret to success")
- 7-10: Large gap ("Why the opposite of what you're doing is correct," "The one thing millionaires do that you're missing")
- Scoring: Does it force them to read? Does it create unresolved tension?

**Dimension 3: Relevance to Target (1-10)**
Is this hook relevant to your specific avatar's biggest problem?
- 1-3: Tangential ("A software tool for business" to an entrepreneur seeking more free time)
- 4-6: Relevant but broad ("Business strategy" for an entrepreneur)
- 7-10: Deeply relevant ("The exact way to reduce business hours to 20/week" for time-starved solopreneur)
- Scoring: Would your specific avatar recognize this as their problem?

**Dimension 4: Emotional Charge (1-10)**
Does it trigger emotional response (not just rational interest)?
- 1-3: No emotion (pure information)
- 4-6: Mild emotion (slight curiosity, mild fear)
- 7-10: Strong emotion (desire to escape pain, FOMO, anger at being lied to)
- Scoring: What feeling does reading it produce? Stronger = higher score.

**Dimension 5: Uniqueness (1-10)**
How many times have people seen this exact hook in this niche?
- 1-3: Extremely common ("How to make $100K," "Ways to lose weight," "How to get clients")
- 4-6: Somewhat common (specific but seen before)
- 7-10: Unique angle (the way it's framed is different from the 100 versions before)
- Scoring: Would it stand out in a competitor's Instagram feed?

### HOOK SCORING EXAMPLES

**Example 1: "Make more money"**
- Specificity: 1 (no numbers, no timeframe)
- Curiosity Gap: 1 (obviously true)
- Relevance: Depends on audience (3-6)
- Emotional Charge: 1 (no emotion)
- Uniqueness: 0 (used 10M times)
- **Total: 1-4/50 — Do Not Use**

**Example 2: "How to generate $10K in new revenue in 90 days using only email"**
- Specificity: 9 (exact number, exact timeframe, specific method)
- Curiosity Gap: 7 (how's this possible without paid ads?)
- Relevance: 8 (extremely relevant to revenue-focused businesses)
- Emotional Charge: 8 (desire + curiosity)
- Uniqueness: 7 (method is somewhat uncommon)
- **Total: 39/50 — Strong Hook, Publish**

**Example 3: "You're probably wasting $40K annually on software you don't use"**
- Specificity: 8 (exact dollar amount)
- Curiosity Gap: 8 (makes reader question their spending)
- Relevance: 9 (perfectly relevant to cost-conscious business owners)
- Emotional Charge: 9 (creates awareness of pain point)
- Uniqueness: 6 (common topic, specific angle)
- **Total: 40/50 — Very Strong Hook, Publish**

### TOTAL SCORE INTERPRETATION

- **41-50: A+ Hook** — Exceptional. Use on everything. 8%+ conversion rate baseline.
- **35-40: A Hook** — Excellent. This will perform well. 5-7% conversion rate baseline.
- **29-34: B Hook** — Good enough. Will work, but not optimal. 3-5% conversion rate baseline.
- **23-28: C Hook** — Mediocre. Rewrite. 1-3% conversion rate baseline.
- **Below 22: F Hook** — Do not use. Return to drawing board.

### QUICK SCORING CHECKLIST

Use this when you don't have time for full scoring:
- [ ] Is there a specific number? (+1)
- [ ] Is there a specific timeframe? (+1)
- [ ] Does it contradict common belief? (+1)
- [ ] Would my ideal customer recognize their pain in it? (+1)
- [ ] Would I stop scrolling to read this? (+1)
- [ ] Have I seen this exact hook before? (-1 if yes)
- [ ] Does it feel emotionally true? (+1)
- [ ] Is there a curiosity gap I need to close? (+1)

Score: 7-8 = Publish. 5-6 = Consider. Below 5 = Rewrite.
